“Wanna See Me Do it Again?”: Dynamic Catch Probability Visualization through ManimGL

Summer 2026

2026 SMT Data Challenge · with Sam Cowan, Evan Kurtz, and Tejas Rama

Our team developed scoreboard-ready replays of Minor League Baseball outfield plays using anonymized ball- and player-tracking data. An XGBoost model estimated catch probability at 20 frames per second, while a custom extension to ManimGL animated 3D player and ball movement alongside probability “clouds.” The replays show fans how a fielder’s chance of making a catch changes over both space and time.

I developed, trained, and evaluated the catch probability models used to generate the probability clouds. I engineered kinematic features and compared logistic regression with XGBoost, considering both predictive performance and how each model represented the nonlinear behavior needed for the animation. This meant evaluating the models for their intended use as a visualization, beyond standard prediction metrics. I revised the modeling pipeline to support rapid experiments and prevent data leakage, and traced unexpected behavior in the probability clouds to a data-integrity issue in the nearest-fielder identification logic. Correcting it improved our Brier score and resolved the animation inconsistencies. I also helped troubleshoot animation issues and write the paper.

Result: our team received an honorable mention in the 2026 SMT Data Challenge.

Save It for Later: Using Backtracking to Value Future ABS Challenge Opportunities

Feb. 2026 – Aug. 2026

Saberseminar 2026 · with Sam Cowan · Boston University Sports Analytics Group

Using an automated ball-strike (ABS) challenge means weighing the current opportunity against the value of having a challenge available later. We developed a backtracking framework to estimate the expected value of future challenge opportunities and use it to quantify the opportunity cost of challenging a pitch.

I designed models to predict league-wide challenge probability and proposed the backtracking algorithm. Together, we developed a metric comparing players’ challenge decisions with league average and estimated the runs those decisions added or cost their teams.

Presented with Sam Cowan at Saberseminar in August 2026.

Tunnel Overlay

Jan. 2026 – May. 2026

Python · OpenCV · scikit-learn

Improved an automated pitch-overlay system for broadcast video through preprocessing and image-registration changes. Replaced baseline cross-correlation with ORB key-point detection and RANSAC homography estimation, and built an end-to-end pipeline that retrieves Baseball Savant video, ranks pitch sequences by trajectory deception, and produces overlays automatically.

Result: improved on baseline image-registration performance by 41% in mean squared error.

OutlyingPitch

Sep. 2025 – Dec. 2025

Python · PyTorch · Streamlit

Built an end-to-end ML pipeline for structured event data, covering preprocessing, feature engineering, model training, and interactive analysis. Features include release point, spin metrics, count context, and handedness.

Result: trained an MLP with 86% top-1 and 97% top-3 accuracy across a 110-pitcher classification task, then used model confusion to create a normalized pitcher similarity metric and Streamlit exploration dashboard.
